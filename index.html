How to Protect Your Data: A Practical Guide

Most data loss and data theft doesn't happen through some sophisticated, unstoppable attack[span_0](start_span)[span_0](end_span). It happens through a handful of predictable gaps[span_1](start_span)[span_1](end_span).

Back up your data - and actually test the backup

This is the single most important thing on this list, and the one most people skip until after they've lost something[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span). The 3-2-1 rule is a reasonable standard to aim for: 3 copies of your data, on 2 different types of storage, with 1 copy kept somewhere physically separate[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span).

Use unique passwords for every account

Reusing a password across multiple accounts means that if one service you use gets breached, an attacker can try that same password on your email, banking, and social media accounts, too[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span). A password manager solves this practically[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span).

Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA)

2FA requires a second piece of verification beyond your password - usually a code from an app, a text message, or a physical security key[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span). Prioritize enabling this on your email account first[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span).

The short version

Protecting your data comes down to a small number of habits done consistently: back up your data and actually test the backup, use unique passwords through a password manager, turn on two-factor authentication, keep software updated, encrypt your devices, and stay alert to phishing[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span).

What Is Ethical Hacking?

Ethical hacking is the practice of deliberately probing computer systems, networks, or applications for security weaknesses - with permission, and with the goal of fixing what's found rather than exploiting it[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span).

Why organizations pay people to hack them

If a vulnerability exists in a piece of software or a network configuration, someone is going to find it eventually[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span). Ethical hackers are hired to find those weaknesses first, document exactly how they got in, and hand that information to the organization so it can be fixed before a real attacker stumbles onto the same gap[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span).

What separates "ethical" from "illegal"

  • Authorization: A written agreement that explicitly permits testing specific systems[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span).
  • Disclosure: Findings go to the organization that owns the system, not to a forum, a buyer, or the public, before the issue is fixed[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span).
  • Non-destructive intent: The goal is to demonstrate that a weakness exists and how it could be exploited, not to cause the damage a real attacker would[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span).
